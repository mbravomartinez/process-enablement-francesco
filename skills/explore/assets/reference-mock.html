Overview

Start here — what this process is for, and where it sits.

Process customer orders and ensure goods are delivered and billed on time.

The three goals
  1. Deliver on the date the customer was promised.
  2. Minimise the manual effort each order costs to process.
  3. Protect revenue that has already been earned but not yet billed.
What goes in, what comes out
Input

A customer's purchase order, arriving by EDI, customer portal or email, stating which materials they want, in what quantity, to which ship-to location, and by which date.

Output

Goods physically delivered to the customer's dock, and an accurate invoice they can pay — which closes as cash in the bank against that order.

Why the organisation depends on it

It is the only path by which demand becomes revenue. Every day an order sits blocked is a day of working capital tied up and a day of service reputation spent.

Where it sits — and what it receives and hands on

Order Management is the middle stage of the Lead to Cash chain. It does not find the customer and it does not chase the money: it takes a won order from the stage before it, and hands a valid receivable to the stage after it. The two arrows below are the handovers — those are where this process is judged.

Terms you'll hear in a workshop
Order block from your context
A system-set hold that stops an order progressing — credit, delivery or billing. It comes up constantly, because most "slow" orders are not slow, they are blocked and unattended.
Goods issue from your context
The posting that records stock physically leaving the warehouse. It matters because it is the moment revenue is earned and the billing clock starts.
Incoterms typical
The contractual split of cost and risk between seller and buyer during transport, such as FOB or DAP. Ask about it whenever freight cost or delivery-date disputes come up.
Touchless order typical
An order that travels from entry to delivery with no manual change. It is the cleanest single proxy for how much effort this process really costs.
DSO from your context
Days Sales Outstanding — the average number of days between invoicing and collecting cash. Finance will frame most of this process in terms of its effect on this number.
OTIF typical
On Time In Full — delivered on the promised date and complete. Worth clarifying early, because teams often measure "on time" and "in full" separately and quote the better one.
